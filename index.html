Problem A scheme that gave women street vendors their own carts had a working 52-cart pilot, then sat for 14 months with no owner.

Read the full caseHide the full case
Approach
Before adding carts, work out what would break at scale and fix that first. Studying the pilot surfaced three gaps: no daily hygiene accountability, a single raw-material supplier, and no vendor training.
My role
I found the scheme during interviews on another topic; nobody had asked us to look at it. I identified the three gaps and proposed a daily geotagged photo check-in to close the first. The team added a second supplier, brought in vendor training and coordinated the relaunch of 24 new carts. A teammate led the pitch.

Problem The client had never run a residential program at this scale, and two batches a week left three days to fix anything before the next group arrived.

Read the full caseHide the full case
Approach
Make every batch inform the next. Feedback started on handwritten Telugu forms that were hard to read and slow to add up. Participants moved to Google Forms, and trainer forms were entered into Excel. From the data, I recommended which trainers to bring back and which sessions needed more time. The client made the final call. Changes that went through included longer sessions and a move from 1.5 to 2 full days.
My role
I was the consulting firm's coordinator. Our work started before the first batch: the client supplied the module content as documents, and we turned it into presentation decks through many rounds of revisions. I built the decks for two to three modules. I then confirmed the master schedule and shared it across the client's teams. During each batch, I directed a 10-member on-site team: collecting attendance signatures, checking every booklet and kit against the count, and calling each absentee to record the reason. After each batch, I turned attendance, absentee reasons and feedback into a report for the client's senior leadership.
Hardest part
The speakers were senior leaders I could not direct, and the client held every final decision. The job was recommending from evidence, not mandating.

Digital Transformation of a Hospital's HVAC Operations

Smart Joules runs HVAC operations for hospitals and commercial buildings.

400+HVAC units across 12 floors
−70%service resolution time
90%portal usage, up from near zero

Resolution time runs from the complaint arriving to the reply that it is resolved.

Problem This was the first site running under Smart Joules' newer model, where it runs a building's entire HVAC operation from commissioning onward rather than auditing an existing system. Other sites under the model were still under construction, so there was no playbook, no written brief and no data on what was going wrong. I was posted to the site, a large private hospital in Hyderabad, to find out and fix it.

Read the full caseHide the full case
Approach
Go to the field before deciding anything. I shadowed day and night shifts, interviewed the operators and the site engineer, and moved three months of logbooks, about 400–500 complaints, into Excel. Two findings drove the plan: a few units caused most complaints, and the morning shift carried most of the volume. After two weeks on site, I turned the verbal mandate into a five-week plan that fixed the data first, then the adoption.
My role
The free text in the prototype was full of spelling mistakes and different wording from each operator, so the same problem was hard to track. I proposed three changes that Smart Joules' tech team built into the Zoho portal: a list of complaint categories to pick from instead of typing, a QR code on each unit to identify the machine on the spot, and a dashboard I designed. I did not write code. I also adapted 15 SOPs with the site engineer.
Hardest part

Getting operators to use it and enter the data correctly. Usage started near zero.

I coached 9 of the 10 operators one-on-one across 8 sessions. I also found that language was limiting use of the written SOPs, so I created audio and video training aids in Hindi and Telugu, which other sites later adopted.

Capacity
I traced the complaint backlog to understaffing and 16-hour shifts and proposed both fixes. Headcount went to 10 and shifts dropped to 12 hours.
Principles

How I Work

Adoption before features
At Smart Joules, I added categories, QR logging and a dashboard to the portal, but features only matter if people use them. Usage started near zero, so I coached operators until they logged complaints with confidence.
Start with the user
The cart scheme surfaced in interviews on another topic. Before relaunching it, I studied the 52-cart pilot for what had worked and what had not, which is where the three gaps came from.
Feedback has to be doable
On the leadership program, we screened every piece of feedback for feasibility before acting on it. Only changes we could implement were taken forward.
