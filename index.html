Smart Joules · Strategy Intern, CEO's Office · April to June 2024

Getting HVAC operators to log complaints in a portal

Smart Joules runs HVAC operations for hospitals and commercial buildings. The question was how to get faster, cleaner complaint data from the people at the units.

Before

Operators noted complaints in a notepad at the unit and copied them into the site register later. The relationship manager typed the register into Excel on top of the day job, and emailed it to the manager when head office asked. Reports could go out a day or two late.

A prototype portal existed, but few operators used it, and those who did typed complaints as free text.

The call

Make logging quicker than the paper chain, and make the data trackable.

The free text in the prototype was full of spelling mistakes and different wording from each operator, so the same problem was hard to track. A list of categories fixes that: the operator picks one instead of typing. A QR code on each unit identifies the machine.

My part
Smart Joules' tech team built the portal in Zoho, starting from a prototype. I proposed three changes that went into it: standardised complaint categories, a QR code on each unit for on-the-spot logging, and a dashboard I designed. Then I drove adoption. I did not write code.
Hardest part

Getting operators to use it and enter the data correctly. Usage started near zero.

I coached operators one-on-one across 8 sessions. I also found that language was limiting use of the written SOPs, so I created audio and video training aids in Hindi and Telugu, which other sites later adopted.

Capacity
I traced the complaint backlog to understaffing and 16-hour shifts and proposed both fixes. Headcount went to 10 and shifts dropped to 12 hours.

Principles

How I work

Adoption before features
At Smart Joules, I added categories, QR logging and a dashboard to the portal, but features only matter if people use them. Usage started near zero, so I coached operators until they logged complaints with confidence.
Start with the user
The cart scheme surfaced during interviews on livelihood opportunities, when one interviewee mentioned it. Before scaling it, we looked at what had worked and what had not for its earlier users.
Feedback has to be doable
On the leadership programme, we screened every piece of feedback for feasibility before acting on it. Only changes we could implement were taken forward.
Small changes, often
I prefer to make small improvements, check the result and adjust, rather than deliver everything at once.
