Read the full caseHide the full case
Before
The client had never run a residential program at this scale. Two batches a week, on Monday and Thursday, left three days to fix anything before the next group arrived.
My part
I was the consulting firm's coordinator. Our work started before the first batch: the client supplied the module content as documents, and we turned it into presentation decks through many rounds of revisions. I built the decks for two to three modules. I then confirmed the master schedule and shared it across the client's teams. During each batch, I directed a 10-member on-site team: collecting attendance signatures, checking every booklet and kit against the count, and calling each absentee to record the reason. After each batch, I turned attendance, absentee reasons and feedback into a report for the client's senior leadership.
Feedback
Feedback started on handwritten Telugu forms that were hard to read and slow to add up. Participants moved to Google Forms, and trainer forms were entered into Excel. From the data, I recommended which trainers to bring back and which sessions needed more time. The client made the final call. Changes that went through included longer sessions and a move from 1.5 to 2 full days.
Hardest part
The speakers were senior leaders I could not direct, and the client held every final decision. The job was recommending from evidence, not mandating.

Before
A scheme giving carts to women street vendors had a 52-cart pilot behind it, then sat for 14 months with no owner.
My part
I came across it during interviews on another topic; nobody had asked us to look at it. Studying the pilot, I found three operational gaps: no daily accountability for hygiene, a single raw-material supplier, and no vendor training. I proposed a daily geotagged photo check-in to close the first.
With the team
Together we added a second supplier, brought in structured vendor training and coordinated the relaunch of 24 new carts. A teammate led the pitch.

Smart Joules · Strategy Intern, CEO's Office · April to June 2024

Getting HVAC Operators to Log Complaints in a Portal

Smart Joules runs HVAC operations for hospitals and commercial buildings. The question was how to get faster, cleaner complaint data from the people at the units.

Service resolution timeDown 70%
Portal usageNear zero to 90%
Reporting stages4 to 2
Manual data entry2.5 hours a day removed, 700+ hours a year

Resolution time runs from the complaint arriving to the reply that it is resolved.

I proposed categories, QR logging and a dashboard for the portal, then coached operators from near-zero usage to 90%.

Before · 4 steps
  1. NotepadOperator notes the complaint at the unit
  2. RegisterCopied into the register later, when free
  3. ExcelRelationship manager types it up
  4. EmailSent to the manager when head office asks
After · 2 steps
  1. PhoneOperator scans the unit's QR code and picks a category
  2. Web portalManager sees it in the portal, no re-typing
Read the full caseHide the full case
Before
Every complaint went through the paper chain above, with the relationship manager doing the typing on top of the day job, so reports could go out a day or two late. A prototype portal existed, but few operators used it, and those who did typed complaints as free text.
My part
The free text in the prototype was full of spelling mistakes and different wording from each operator, so the same problem was hard to track. I proposed three changes that Smart Joules' tech team built into the Zoho portal: a list of complaint categories to pick from instead of typing, a QR code on each unit to identify the machine on the spot, and a dashboard I designed. I did not write code.
Hardest part

Getting operators to use it and enter the data correctly. Usage started near zero.

I coached 9 of the 10 operators one-on-one across 8 sessions. I also found that language was limiting use of the written SOPs, so I created audio and video training aids in Hindi and Telugu, which other sites later adopted.

Capacity
I traced the complaint backlog to understaffing and 16-hour shifts and proposed both fixes. Headcount went to 10 and shifts dropped to 12 hours.
Principles

How I Work

Adoption before features
At Smart Joules, I added categories, QR logging and a dashboard to the portal, but features only matter if people use them. Usage started near zero, so I coached operators until they logged complaints with confidence.
Start with the user
The cart scheme surfaced in interviews on another topic. Before relaunching it, I studied the 52-cart pilot for what had worked and what had not, which is where the three gaps came from.
Feedback has to be doable
On the leadership program, we screened every piece of feedback for feasibility before acting on it. Only changes we could implement were taken forward.
